A dry day has less than 1.0 mm of precipitation (the standard ETCCDI definition), so trace amounts don't break a spell. A missing or invalid day always ends a dry spell — gaps in old records can shorten historical spells but can never fabricate them. Driest-stretch windows require every day valid and are non-overlapping, like the extreme-precipitation events. Annual charts include only years with at least 300 days of valid data; spells are credited to the year they end in. The seasonal chart uses each spell's midpoint month and splits the record at its midpoint year.

A caution on the earliest records: 19th-century observers often under-recorded light winter snowfall, and a day logged as 0.0 mm counts as dry here. Cold-season dry spells from a station's earliest decades may therefore be inflated — treat winter entries from that era with more skepticism than summer ones, and than anything from the modern instrument record.
